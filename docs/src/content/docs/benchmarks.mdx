## TabArena

[TabArena](https://tabarena.ai) v0.1 `lite`: all 51 tasks (13 regression, 30 binary, 8 multiclass),
one outer split per task, 8-fold bagging and AutoGluon preprocessing.

- **Who was trained:** only miter. The LightGBM, CatBoost and XGBoost results are TabArena's own
  published runs, with up to 200 configurations each; miter used 25.
- **What is compared:** each method at three levels:
  - default parameters;
  - tuned, meaning the best configuration on validation;
  - tuned + ensemble, meaning a weighted ensemble of configurations.

Per-task paired comparison on the same 51 tasks. Wins count tasks where miter's error is lower,
the median is miter's relative error difference (negative = miter better), and p is from a
Wilcoxon signed-rank test:

<div class="lg-table">

| miter vs | default | tuned | tuned + ensemble |
|---|---|---|---|
| CatBoost | 24/51, +0.31%, p = 0.40 | 31/51, −0.44%, p = 0.44 | 35/51, −0.78%, p = 0.14 |
| LightGBM | 41/51, −3.23%, p < 0.001 | 39/51, −0.99%, p = 0.003 | 37/51, −0.62%, p = 0.01 |
| XGBoost | 37/51, −1.50%, p = 0.01 | 37/51, −1.11%, p = 0.003 | 38/51, −1.27%, p < 0.001 |

</div>

What this shows:
- **LightGBM and XGBoost.** miter is ahead of both at every level, and the per-task tests are
  significant.
- **CatBoost.** miter is level at every level. It has the higher Elo once tuned, but no per-task
  difference is statistically significant.
- **Tuned miter without the ensemble** (Elo 1176) is above CatBoost and LightGBM *with* their
  ensembles.

On the full TabArena leaderboard (121 methods), miter tuned + ensemble is #35. That is above every
GBDT variant, but below the tabular foundation models and the deep-learning ensembles at the top.

## Single configurations

Each bar is one configuration with no tuning, scored the same way as the `default` rows above.

## Held-out OpenML data

The configurations above were developed while looking at TabArena. To check that they generalise
rather than fit the benchmark, we ran them on OpenML datasets with **no overlap** with TabArena or
with any earlier experiment. The protocol was 5-fold cross-validation with early stopping.
These are the results that made symmetric trees the default.

<div class="lg-table">

| comparison (32 datasets, 5-fold CV) | wins | median | p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| miter default vs CatBoost default | 23/32 | −1.59% | 0.005 |
| miter default vs LightGBM default | 28/32 | −6.99% | < 0.001 |
| miter default vs miter leaf-wise (default before v1.0) | 27/32 | −3.17% | < 0.001 |

</div>

On learning curves of 8 further held-out datasets, symmetric miter had a 3–8% lower median error
than LightGBM at every training size from 300 to 16,000 rows. It also beat CatBoost on 5–6 of 8
datasets from 3,000 rows up.

## Training and prediction speed

**With early stopping**, on the 32 held-out datasets (one thread, Apple M4), the default trained in
a median 1.7 s per dataset:
- CatBoost took 3.3 s, so miter was 0.41× CatBoost's total time;
- LightGBM took 0.2 s, so miter was 7.7× LightGBM's total time.

miter stops after about 580 trees (median), CatBoost after 1130 and LightGBM after 165.

**Where the time goes.**
- **Oblique candidates.** About 70% of miter's training time goes into them; with axis splits alone,
  miter trains as fast as LightGBM. `preset="fast"` keeps oblique splits near the root only and
  halves the time.
- **Binary columns.** Data with many binary (one-hot) columns is the slow case, because every column
  enters the oblique pool. covertype (581k rows, 44 of 54 columns binary) takes 57 s for 100 trees
  on one thread, compared with CatBoost's 8.2 s and LightGBM's 1.6 s.

**Prediction** runs on lignum's binned kernel. On one thread of an Apple M4 with 300 trees, it takes
15.8 ns per row and tree; LightGBM's own `predict` takes 19.3 ns, and lignum's compiled mode 4.3 ns.
